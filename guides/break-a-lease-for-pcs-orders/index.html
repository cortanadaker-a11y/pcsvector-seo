Breaking a lease for PCS orders

If your orders say you are leaving, your rental lease does not have to follow you. This guide summarizes the federal rule from the Department of Justice and the notes housing offices give new arrivals. It is general information, not legal advice; your installation’s legal assistance office is the place to ask how it applies to your lease. (U.S. Department of Justice, Financial and Housing Rights (SCRA))

What the SCRA says about residential leases

  • The SCRA lets a servicemember end a residential lease after receiving PCS orders, or deployment orders for at least 90 days. The lease must be signed by someone who later enters service, or by someone in service who later gets PCS or deployment orders. Separation or retirement orders also qualify. (U.S. Department of Justice, Financial and Housing Rights (SCRA))
  • How: give the landlord written notice and a copy of your orders (or a letter from your commanding officer). DOJ says the notice may be sent electronically as well as by mail or hand delivery. (U.S. Department of Justice, Financial and Housing Rights (SCRA))
  • When it ends: if you pay rent monthly, the lease terminates 30 days after the next rent payment is due once you have given proper notice and orders. That is not the same as 30 days from the day you send the notice. (U.S. Department of Justice, Financial and Housing Rights (SCRA))
  • DOJ’s position: requiring a servicemember to repay rent concessions or discounts is an early-termination fee that violates the SCRA, and mileage requirements in a lease are likely unenforceable because the SCRA has no minimum distance to the new station. (U.S. Department of Justice, Financial and Housing Rights (SCRA))
  • Waivers: the SCRA allows a waiver only as a separate instrument, during or after the relevant service period, in at least 12-point type. DOJ says waivers are invalid if they are lease addenda signed with the lease and give you nothing in return. (U.S. Department of Justice, Financial and Housing Rights (SCRA))

What installation offices add

  • Fort Drum: most landlords require a one-year lease, so insist on a military clause and have the Housing Services Office or Legal Assistance Office review it before you sign. (Military OneSource, Fort Drum housing)
  • Fort Hood: make sure the lease contains the SCRA clause, and complete and keep a signed move-in inspection checklist to avoid repair or penalty charges when you leave. (Fort Hood’s page describes the clause as covering orders away for more than 90 days; DOJ’s summary above is the legal source, so ask a legal assistance office how it applies to you.) (Military OneSource, Fort Hood housing; U.S. Department of Justice, Financial and Housing Rights (SCRA))
  • Fort Riley: accepting on-post housing is not, by itself, justification to break a lease. If you hold deployment orders, call the Off Post Housing Division for help with the process. (Military OneSource, Fort Riley housing)
  • Camp Humphreys: the garrison housing booklet says the lease will contain a military clause and that the service member or DA civilian gives 30 days’ written notice on receipt of orders or an offer of government quarters. The DOJ summary above does not list an offer of quarters as an SCRA ground, so treat that line as the garrison’s own guidance and confirm it with the legal assistance office. (Humphreys Housing Services Office booklet; U.S. Department of Justice, Financial and Housing Rights (SCRA))
  • Germany: the Wiesbaden briefing says verbal agreements with a landlord or agent are binding and that termination needs written notice with a 3-month period, so read the termination terms before you sign. (USAG Wiesbaden off-post housing briefing (Apr 2022))

Steps in order

  1. Get a copy of your PCS orders (or a commander’s letter).
  2. Write a short termination notice naming the lease, the address and the effective date you are asking for; keep a dated copy.
  3. Deliver it to the landlord or agent by a method you can prove (mail, hand delivery or email if the landlord accepts it).
  4. Calculate the end date: 30 days after the next rent due date after delivery, for monthly rent.
  5. Do a move-out inspection with the landlord and keep photos of the condition you leave the home in.

The steps are a plain-language outline of the DOJ summary above, not a legal template.

Quick answers

Does 30 days’ notice start the day I send it?

No. For a monthly lease, DOJ says the lease ends 30 days after the next rent payment is due following delivery of your notice and a copy of your orders.

Can a landlord charge an early-termination fee when I PCS?

DOJ’s position is that a covered servicemember who properly terminates under the SCRA may not be charged an early-termination fee, and that requiring repayment of rent concessions or discounts is such a fee. Ask your legal assistance office if a landlord pushes back.

Can I end a lease if I take on-post housing?

Not automatically. Fort Riley’s housing information says accepting on-post housing is not justification to break a lease or rental agreement, and the DOJ summary lists orders (PCS, deployment of 90 days or more, separation or retirement), not a housing offer. Garrisons differ: the Camp Humphreys booklet lists an offer of government quarters as a reason for notice, so ask your legal assistance office about your lease.
